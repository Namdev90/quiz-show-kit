# Research and design the questions

The assistant owns question drafting. The teacher may supply a complete question list, goals, corrections or preferred material; no attachment is required to begin. **Supplied and AI-researched questions pass through the same independent checks.** Start with a blueprint that maps each round to learning objectives, topics, cognitive demand and media needs. Use matched pairs to give both teams comparable primary opportunities.

## Pick sources for the subject

This is a source-selection guide, not a ready-verified bibliography. Access the actual materials before citing them. A search snippet, generated answer, anonymous question bank or AI summary is not sufficient evidence for a scored answer.

| Subject or task | Start with | Use for cross-checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course-specific quiz | Teacher's syllabus, learning outcomes, supplied notes and assigned textbook | The original textbook/reference and relevant authoritative sources; resolve errors in notes openly |
| Established science | Authoritative textbooks, university teaching resources and recognized scientific institutions | Original papers for disputed, specific or newly developed claims |
| Laboratory methods | Original method, official protocol, instrument manufacturer's manual or standard-setting body's current method | Validation studies and appropriate textbook explanation; keep method/version conditions explicit |
| New research | Original peer-reviewed publication and linked dataset/methods | Independent replication or systematic review; label preprints and unresolved findings |
| Computing or engineering | Official documentation, specifications, original standards and maintainers' material | A reproducible example or original paper; state version-dependent behavior |
| History, literature or languages | Primary text, original record, archive, critical edition | Scholarly reference and academic commentary; avoid presenting disputed readings as one unquestioned fact |
| Geography, public data or current affairs | Responsible public institution, original dataset or official announcement | Independent high-quality reporting; name the date and geography |
| Law, health or regulation | Applicable official legal/regulatory text or current professional guidance | Primary research and recognized institutional references; keep jurisdiction, version and scope explicit |
| Theme inspiration | Teacher references, original design portfolios, museums or openly licensed collections | Check authorship, license and suitability; an inspiration image is not scientific evidence |

For foundational content, one authoritative source may support a straightforward item. Use a second independent source for ambiguity, high-stakes claims, recent changes or disputed terminology. Search more only where it resolves a concrete uncertainty. Do not treat the number of citations as a substitute for source quality.

If internet/source access is absent, draft only what can be supported by readable supplied material and identify unresolved verification. Never fabricate a URL or a source review. Use actual source-review dates, not the date a copied bank happened to be built. A supplied private document can be cited by title without publishing it or inventing a public link.

For each question, inspect support for the stem, intended answer, every distractor/explanation, units, definitions, population/context and relevant date. A true statement can still be the wrong answer to a narrowly qualified stem. Record title, direct HTTPS URL where available, page/section, edition/publication date where relevant, actual access/verification date, verification status and a concise original support note. Check that the linked page is the one inspected and supports this claim; do not cite a search result as if its destination was read. Mark stale, inaccessible or conflicting evidence for review.

If evidence disagrees with a supplied key, explain the exact conflict and offer **correct, replace, omit, or keep the creator's version**. Record an explicit keep decision as **creator override; verification disagreement remains**, with the chosen key and unresolved issue in host/editor metadata. Never manufacture a citation, label the item independently verified or silently rewrite its educational meaning. Missing IDs, invalid keys and unsafe media remain blocking structural errors. A review skip is not an override decision.

Use preferably one primary source beneath the revealed answer and expandable additional references only as needed. All four options need a concise explanation of why they are correct/incorrect in the stated context. Bibliographic records and original notes remain available offline; websites may not. Do not bundle copyrighted articles without rights. Keep source titles/URLs, answer notes, informative filenames, captions, alt text and preloaded narration from revealing the key before answer reveal.

## Make the difficulty defensible

Difficulty is relative to this audience and its preparation. Use the existing labels `accessible`, `moderate` and `challenging`, or a consistent supported custom label. The cognitive rubric below guides authoring; it does not claim that difficulty has been measured in real students.

| Label | Demand for this audience | Suitable laboratory example pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accessible** | Recognize or explain one taught concept with familiar wording | Identify the purpose of a labeled apparatus or control |
| **Moderate** | Apply a concept to a new but brief situation, or interpret one display | Choose an appropriate control or infer the meaning of one result |
| **Challenging** | Integrate two or more taught ideas, discriminate plausible explanations or evaluate a limitation | Diagnose a methodological inconsistency from a small, fully specified dataset |

Do not manufacture difficulty with obscure trivia, double negatives, dense reading, microscopic image labels or ambiguous assumptions. Do not confuse a short response timer with higher cognitive demand. Give paired questions comparable reading length, visual complexity and number of reasoning steps as well as the same label.

Propose a balanced mix matched to the lesson, then accept a custom distribution. For a new mixed cohort, a starting suggestion is 40% accessible, 40% moderate and 20% challenging; round to **complete pairs**, disclose the resulting counts, and adjust from teacher feedback. Do not force those percentages onto very small banks or a different learning objective.

## Ideation patterns

Use a varied set of prompts: identify a labeled component; select an essential control; interpret a simple graph/table; choose the next conceptual step; compare two methods; identify the scope of a conclusion; spot a confounder; evaluate a source. For laboratory-focused MSc work, favor methods, observations, quality control and interpretation over elaborate clinical cases unless explicitly requested.

For each item, choose the objective and defensible answer first. Write three distractors from distinct, plausible misconceptions. Keep the four options grammatically parallel and reasonably similar in length. Avoid accidental cues, overlapping answers, unsupported absolutes, trick wording and unnecessary “all/none of the above”. If multiple answers remain defensible, revise or replace the question.

Use original concise wording and explanations. Record sources supporting both the answer and any necessary qualification. Scientific visuals must be accurate, labeled consistently, legible and credited; use deterministic diagrams/plots for exact relationships and data. Decorative generated images must not serve as evidence.

Offer the creator a content mix: text; numerical calculation with units in the options; image/diagram; graph/data interpretation; code/output interpretation; sequence-as-MCQ; match-pairs-as-MCQ. Every type still uses four selectable options and one intended key. For sequence/mapping options, keep internal labels meaningful when the outer options shuffle. No numeric entry, open response, drag-and-drop ordering or matching interface is implied. Let the creator choose the mix; do not force media into questions that benefit from plain text.

## Bank acceptance before build

Check that every main/tie pair is complete and comparable, no paired explanation gives away its partner, all rounds have the required even count, and correct positions are reasonably balanced without a predictable sequence. Reserves must fit the topic/difficulty of the slot they replace. Keep practice unscored and separate. Audit the total bank, per-team opportunities and short-mode pair selection.

Review the **whole sequence**, not only neighboring pairs: question stems, diagrams, options, source titles and revealed explanations may disclose a later answer. Move, revise or replace leaking items; do not conceal uncertainty with a claim that automation found every leak. Compare normalized stems and conceptual objectives for exact/near repeats, including practice, reserves and tie cards. Decide whether an intentional repeated concept serves revision and record why.

Compare the planned topic/objective coverage with the actual inventory and per-team opportunities, including short mode. Report gaps, unintended concentration and similar questions under different wording. Rising difficulty and reverse/falling difficulty are separate choices; manual/mixed preserves intentional ordering. Change complete pairs and check balance rather than independently sorting one team's questions. Difficulty is creator-assigned, not a measured universal score.

Respect the current field limits: stems up to 300 characters, four distinct options up to 180 characters each, numeric correct index 0–3, explanations up to 450 characters, supported source records and actual verification dates. Preserve stable question/option identities; the [canonical configuration](../CONFIGURATION.md) defines the current metadata fields and remaining limits. Validate the complete event rather than individual excerpts alone.

Deliver a compact question-quality report with columns **question ID · issue/evidence · proposed action · status**. Include checked references/dates, missing option explanations, ambiguous units, source disagreements, explicit overrides, topic coverage, near repeats and leakage decisions. Separate blocking structural failures from warnings and pending factual review. Summarize counts and only actionable items; no fake certainty/quality percentage. Record the scope actually checked and anything source access prevented. Use [the bank workflow](QUESTION_BANK.md) for safe DOCX changes and optional print export.

Offer a concise question/answer/source review once. A teacher can skip review; continue independent checks and label the skip honestly. Never re-open an already skipped review loop merely to satisfy a template. A passing build checks structure, not the truth of an answer.
